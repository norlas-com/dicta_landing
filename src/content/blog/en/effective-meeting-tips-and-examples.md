> **Interesting fact.** In a Harvard Business Review study, 182 senior managers across industries were surveyed: 71% called meetings unproductive and inefficient, 65% said meetings keep them from completing their own work, and 64% said meetings come at the expense of deep thinking. Yet these same managers spend a large part of their week in meetings.

Meetings are not the problem. Meetings without a goal, without an agenda and without an outcome are. The good news is that this is easy to fix. Below are the rules, templates and examples you can apply as early as next week.

## What makes a meeting effective

An effective meeting answers four questions before it starts:

1. **Why are we meeting?** Is there a specific decision to make or information to discuss?
2. **Who is needed?** Only the people who make the decision or without whom it cannot be made.
3. **What is the output?** A decision, a plan, a list of tasks with owners and deadlines.
4. **Is this meeting needed at all?** If the issue can be solved by email or a chat message, cancel the meeting.

## Tips before the meeting

### 1. State the goal in one sentence

Weak: "Discuss marketing."\
Strong: "Choose the channel for the November promotion and assign owners."

### 2. Prepare an agenda

Everyone needs an agenda, even for a 15-minute stand-up. It should include:

- the topic and goal of the meeting;
- the discussion items, with time for each;
- who leads each item;
- what to read or prepare in advance.

**Sample agenda (60 minutes):**

| Time | Item | Owner | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Goal and recap of the last meeting | Facilitator | Everyone has the context |
| 5–25 min | Discuss launch channel options | Marketer | List of pros and cons |
| 25–45 min | Budget and timeline | Finance manager | Agreed figures |
| 45–55 min | Decision and task assignment | Facilitator | Tasks with owners |
| 55–60 min | Summary and next steps | Facilitator | Short recap |

### 3. Limit the number of attendees

The more people there are, the less involved each one is. Invite those who make the decision or are responsible for delivery. The others can get a summary afterwards.

### 4. Pick the right length

Don't default to "an hour". If the issue takes 20 minutes, schedule 20 minutes. Many experts recommend 25 or 50 minutes instead of 30 and 60, to leave a break between meetings.

### 5. Send materials in advance

If participants need to read a document, send it ahead and ask them to review it before the meeting. Then the meeting is spent on discussion, not on reading.

## Tips during the meeting

### 6. Start on time

If you wait for latecomers, you penalize those who arrived on time. Start exactly on schedule and restate the goal briefly.

### 7. Assign roles

- **Facilitator** keeps to the agenda and the time.
- **Note-taker** records decisions and tasks (or an AI assistant does).
- **Timekeeper** reminds everyone of the schedule.

### 8. Stick to the agenda

If an important but unrelated topic comes up, write it in a "parking lot" and return to it separately. This keeps the discussion from drifting.

### 9. Let everyone speak

Ask quiet participants: "What do you think about this option?" A short round of opinions before a decision often surfaces risks that would otherwise go unnoticed.

### 10. Say decisions out loud

When a decision is made, state it: "So, we launch in November via Instagram and Telegram, owner Dilnoza, deadline Friday." This gives everyone the same understanding.

### 11. Finish with time to spare

Leave the last 5 minutes for a recap: what was decided, who does what, and by when.

## Tips after the meeting

### 12. Send the summary within 24 hours

The later you send the minutes, the less they are remembered and the less likely the agreements are to be carried out.

**Sample short meeting minutes:**

> **Meeting:** November promotion launch\
> **Date:** November 5\
> **Participants:** Facilitator, marketer, finance manager, sales manager
>
> **Decisions:**
> 1. The promotion launches on November 15.
> 2. Main channels: Instagram and Telegram.
> 3. The budget is approved within the agreed amount.
>
> **Tasks:**
> - Prepare creatives: marketer, by November 10.
> - Agree promotion terms: sales manager, by November 8.
> - Set up a results report: finance manager, by November 12.
>
> **Next meeting:** November 12, preparation results.

### 13. Assign owners and deadlines

A task without a name and a deadline doesn't get done. The formula: **what + who + by when**.

### 14. Follow up on completion

Start the next meeting by checking the tasks from the previous one. It builds discipline and shows that agreements matter.

## Examples of meeting formats

- **Daily stand-up, 10–15 minutes.** Everyone briefly answers: what I did yesterday, what I'm doing today, what's blocking me. No discussions, in turn.
- **Weekly team meeting, 30–45 minutes.** Results of the week, priorities, blockers.
- **Decision meeting, 30–60 minutes.** One problem, several options, a decision at the end.
- **Brainstorm, 45–60 minutes.** Ideas first without criticism, then selection and evaluation.
- **Retrospective, 45–60 minutes.** What worked, what didn't, what we change.
- **One-on-one, 20–30 minutes.** Feedback, development, the employee's questions.

An example often cited in discussions about work habits: according to media reports, in 2023 Shopify's chief operating officer cancelled all recurring meetings with more than three attendees. For many teams, it was a prompt to re-examine which meetings are really needed.

## Common mistakes

- No goal and no agenda.
- Everyone is invited "just in case".
- The meeting turns into a presentation that could have been an email.
- Decisions are not recorded, and a week later everyone remembers them differently.
- No owners and no deadlines.
- The minutes arrive days later, or never.

## Effective meeting checklist

**Before:**
- [ ] The goal is stated in one sentence
- [ ] The meeting is really needed
- [ ] The agenda and materials have been sent
- [ ] Only the necessary people are invited

**During:**
- [ ] Started on time
- [ ] Roles assigned
- [ ] Sticking to the agenda
- [ ] Decisions stated out loud

**After:**
- [ ] The summary was sent within 24 hours
- [ ] Every task has an owner and a deadline
- [ ] The next meeting starts by checking the tasks

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a meeting be?**\
As long as the goal requires. For a stand-up it is 10–15 minutes, for discussion and a decision usually 30–60 minutes.

**How many people should I invite?**\
Only those who make the decision or are responsible for delivery. Send the summary to everyone else.

**How do I avoid meetings that run long?**\
An agenda with time limits, a timekeeper and a "parking lot" for off-topic items.

**How can I write meeting minutes quickly?**\
Record decisions and tasks in a "what, who, deadline" structure, or use an AI assistant that makes the summary automatically.
