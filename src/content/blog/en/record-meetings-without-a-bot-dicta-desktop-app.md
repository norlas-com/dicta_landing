You know the moment. A client joins the call, sees "Meeting Recorder Bot has joined", and the mood changes. Someone asks who invited it. Someone else asks whether the call is being sent somewhere. Ten minutes of trust-building start from a small deficit.

**Dicta's desktop app solves this by recording the meeting from your own computer.** No bot joins the call, no extra participant appears in the room, and you still get a transcript by speaker and an AI summary afterwards. The [desktop app](/en/features/desktop-app/) is available for **macOS and Windows**, works with **Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams**, and detects your call automatically.

## What is botless meeting recording?

There are two ways to record an online meeting:

1. **A bot joins the meeting.** A recording participant enters the call, records it, and leaves. Everyone sees it in the participant list.
2. **An app records on your device.** The app on your computer captures the meeting from your side. Nothing joins the call.

The second approach is what people mean by *botless* or *on-device* recording, and it is what Dicta's desktop app does.

## How Dicta's desktop app works

1. **Download the app** from the [Dicta download page](/en/download/) and sign in.
2. **Join your meeting as usual** in Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams. You keep using the tools you already use for calls.
3. **Dicta detects the call and shows a small Record button** over your meeting. One click starts recording from your computer. There is no bot, so nothing changes on the other participants' screens. When the call ends, Dicta stops and uploads the recording by itself.
4. **Get the result.** The recording is sent to Dicta for transcription with speaker labels, and the AI summary appears in your account with topics, decisions and tasks.
5. **Find it anywhere.** Meetings sync across the web, iOS and Android.


## Why it is so useful

### No awkward moment

The biggest benefit is human, not technical. Clients, candidates, partners and senior colleagues are more relaxed when the room is just the people they expected. A bot name in the participant list can raise questions you do not want to spend time answering.

### Automatic call detection

Dicta recognizes when you join a call in Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams and puts a Record button right over the meeting, so there is nothing to set up for each call: one click and it records. That also helps with unplanned calls: the quick chat that turns into an important one is less likely to go unrecorded because you forgot to prepare a recorder.

### Works when a bot would be a problem

Some meetings are hard for bots: calls you are a guest in, where you cannot or do not want to ask the host to admit a third-party participant, or organizations that restrict outside participants. Recording on your own computer avoids that friction.

### Nothing extra to manage

There is no bot to invite, name, admit or remove. You join your call, click Record, and the app does the rest.

### Same result as a bot-based recording

You still get a transcript with speaker labels in **Uzbek, Russian and English**, and an AI summary with decisions and tasks, plus the Dicta features that come with it: searchable text, AI chat across your past meetings, tasks you can send to Jira, Trello, Asana or ClickUp, and summaries in Telegram.

### One account across devices

Meetings recorded on your computer show up alongside the ones you record on your phone or import as files.

## Consent still matters

Because nothing visibly joins the call, other participants will not see a sign that it is being recorded. A recording tool that does not announce itself makes it more important that you do.

- **Tell participants** at the start: "I'll record this to make notes, is that okay?"
- **Follow the law and your organization's rules.** Where consent to record is required, getting it is your responsibility.
- **Share the summary** afterwards. It builds trust and keeps everyone aligned.

## Tips for the best result

- **Use good audio.** A decent microphone or headset gives a cleaner transcript.
- **Let people speak one at a time** where possible.
- **Introduce participants** at the start so names are clear.
- **Allow microphone and audio access** the first time, when your system asks for it.
