After an online meeting, the same scene tends to repeat: "Who promised what?", "What did we decide about pricing?", "Is there a recording?" Zoom and Google Meet both offer built-in recording, but a recording is not the same as text, decisions, and action items. A 40-minute video file is something nobody has time to rewatch.

This article walks through a practical way to record, transcribe, and summarize Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams meetings automatically: how a meeting bot (an AI notetaker) works and how to set one up.

## What is a meeting bot?

A meeting bot is a program that joins the call as a regular participant. It listens and records on your behalf, then prepares a transcript and summary when the meeting ends. All you do is give it the meeting link.

The bot approach has a practical advantage: there is nothing to install, either on your computer or for the other participants. The bot stays in the meeting even if your computer is off or your internet connection drops.

## How it differs from the platform's own recording

Zoom and Google Meet have their own recording features, but with limits:

- often only the host, or someone the host has authorized, can start a recording;
- the result is a video file that you cannot easily search;
- what transcription and summary you get depends on the platform and your plan, and for Uzbek it is usually limited (check the official documentation for the exact situation);
- decisions and action items are not pulled out automatically.

A bot fills that gap: the recording, a speaker-labeled transcript, and an AI summary in one place.

## Things to watch out for with a meeting bot

**Tell people.** The bot appears in the meeting under its own name, so it is not hidden. Even so, it is good practice to say at the start, "This meeting is being recorded." This matters most in meetings with clients and external partners.

**Permissions.** Some organizations restrict Zoom, Meet, or Teams so external participants cannot join automatically. In that case the host has to admit the bot manually or change the setting.

**Audio quality.** Transcript quality depends on sound quality. Keep the microphone close, avoid two people talking at once, and do not join from a noisy place.

**Review the output.** An AI summary is an excellent first draft, but check numbers, names, and deadlines yourself before copying them into an important document.

## Bot or desktop app?

- **Bot:** convenient for joining someone else's meeting with just a link, for when your computer is busy or off, and for recording team meetings in one central place.
- **Desktop app** (macOS and Windows): records meetings on your computer automatically. This is the better option if you would rather not have a bot visibly join the call.
