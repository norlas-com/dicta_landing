"Which app records meetings best in Uzbek?" There is no single right answer, because these tools were built for different jobs. Below we compare Dicta, Aisha AI, Evernote, and Copy AI from the point of view of your workflow. A disclosure: we are the Dicta team, so we are writing about our own product. That is why we state the strengths of the others openly and do not guess at things we do not know for certain.

> Note: products change quickly. Before you decide, check each service's official website for current pricing, language support, and features.

## First, what do you actually need?

Behind the single phrase "turn speech into text" sit at least four different needs:

1. **Transcribing a short voice message or clip.** One person speaks and you want text.
2. **Meeting transcription.** Several people speak, and who said what, the decisions, and the action items all matter.
3. **Personal notes and a knowledge base.** Notes, documents, and articles in one place.
4. **Writing new text.** Marketing copy, posts, or emails.

Each tool is strongest at one of these. A wrong choice usually comes from a mismatch with the task, not from a bad product.

## Aisha AI

Aisha AI is known for AI products built for the Uzbek language (developed by Voicelab). It has local experience in understanding Uzbek and converting speech to text, and a team working in the language is a clear strength. If what you mainly need is Uzbek speech-to-text or a voice solution tailored to the local language, Aisha AI is definitely worth a look.

Which capabilities are available, such as separating speakers in multi-person meetings, extracting action items automatically, or sending a bot into Zoom and Google Meet, can vary by plan. We suggest checking this yourself on the official site or through a demo.

## Evernote

Evernote is not a meeting recorder. It is a note-taking and knowledge-storage tool. Its strengths are organizing and searching notes, clipping web pages, and keeping documents and images in one place. For people who have used it for years, it works well as a personal archive.

You can attach audio to a note in Evernote, but producing meeting minutes or a speaker-separated transcript is not its main purpose. If you need a notebook, it is a good choice. If your goal is to record a meeting and get decisions and action items out of it, it makes more sense to pair it with a specialized tool.

## Copy AI

Copy AI is mainly a text-generation tool, designed for marketing copy, posts, emails, and similar content. It helps you write, but recording and transcribing meetings is not its central function. Comparing it with a meeting recorder is like comparing apples and oranges.

If you need a customer email or ad copy, a tool like this is useful. For meeting minutes you need something built for meetings. The two can sit side by side in the same workflow.

## Dicta

Dicta is built specifically for meetings. Every path ends in a recording, a speaker-labeled transcript, and an AI summary (topics, decisions, action items). What it offers:

- **In-person meetings:** record a real meeting on your phone and separate speakers by voice.
- **Round table:** each person records on their own phone, and Dicta merges the recordings.
- **Meeting bot:** send a bot to a Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams meeting.
- **Dicta meeting room:** online meetings in the browser and in the apps.
- **File upload:** transcribe existing audio or video.
- **Languages:** Uzbek, Russian, English, and Arabic.
- **Action items:** tasks extracted from the meeting, export to tools such as Trello, and summaries delivered via Telegram.
- **Apps:** web, iOS, Android, and desktop.

There are limits too. Dicta is not a notebook and not a text generator; it is focused on meetings. Like any automatic transcription, it loses accuracy in noisy rooms, when people talk over each other, and with strong dialects, so important documents should be reviewed.

## Global tools

If you work outside the Uzbek-language context, Otter, Fireflies, tl;dv, and Zoom's built-in AI features are the usual global options for meeting notes. Check their Uzbek-language support yourself before relying on them for Uzbek audio, using your own recordings rather than the marketing page.

## How to choose

- **Multi-person meetings, minutes, and action items:** a meeting-focused tool such as Dicta.
- **Mostly Uzbek speech-to-text, or a local voice solution:** try Aisha AI and compare it on your own audio.
- **A personal archive of notes and documents:** Evernote.
- **Marketing copy or emails:** a text generator such as Copy AI.

The most reliable method is to test with your own audio. Run the same 10-minute meeting recording through two or three tools and compare Uzbek accuracy, speaker separation, and summary quality. Signing up for Dicta is free, so you lose nothing by trying it.
